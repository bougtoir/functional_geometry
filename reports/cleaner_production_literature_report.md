# Cleaner-production literature map for functional geometry optimization

**Role:** cleaner-production literature specialist
**Project:** *Redesign Before Substitution: Functional Geometry Optimization for Reducing Material Use in Disposable Products*
**Access date for all web sources:** **2026-09-24**
**Evidence status:** bibliographic metadata are verified against saved Crossref responses; substantive statements below are limited to information checked in official pages, abstracts, or accessible full text. Metadata verification does not imply full-text verification of every cited paper.

## 1. Executive assessment

### Verified

1. The project sits inside established **cleaner production**, **eco-design**, **material efficiency**, **source reduction**, **waste prevention**, and **narrowing resource flows**. Cleaner production is explicitly preventive and applies an integrated strategy to processes, products, and services; product redesign that avoids material input before waste arises is therefore a legitimate upstream cleaner-production intervention, whereas recycling and disposal are downstream or complementary strategies rather than synonyms ([UNEP/UNIDO cleaner-production definition](https://www.un.org/esa/sustdev/sdissues/technology/cleanerproduction.pdf); [US Pollution Prevention Act/EPA](https://www.epa.gov/p2/pollution-prevention-act-1990); [Bocken et al. 2016, DOI:10.1080/21681015.2016.1172124](https://doi.org/10.1080/21681015.2016.1172124)).

2. The closest established umbrella is **material efficiency: providing the same material service with less material production/input**, supported by strategies including designing products with less material, reducing yield losses, longer life, reuse, remanufacturing, and more intensive use ([Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002); [Allwood et al. 2013, DOI:10.1098/rsta.2012.0496](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2012.0496)).

3. **MIPS (Material Input per Service Unit)** is the closest established denominator-level analogue to the project's material-per-function logic. MIPS normalizes resource input by a delivered service unit, while the proposed study would specialize this logic to supplied versus functionally feasible product geometry under explicit constraints ([Liedtke et al. 2014, DOI:10.3390/resources3030544](https://doi.org/10.3390/resources3030544); [Ritthoff et al., *Calculating MIPS*](https://epub.wupperinst.org/frontdoor/deliver/index/docId/1577/file/WS27e.pdf)).

4. **Functional Geometry Optimization (FGO)** was not identified as an established cleaner-production or eco-design label in targeted exact-phrase searches. The exact labels **Geometry Utilization Efficiency (GUE)** and **Geometric Efficiency Factor (GEF)** were likewise not identified as established general sustainability metrics in the searched bibliographic sources ([Crossref query: “Functional Geometry Optimization”](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=%22Functional%20Geometry%20Optimization%22&rows=20); [Crossref query: “Geometry Utilization Efficiency”](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=%22Geometry%20Utilization%20Efficiency%22&rows=20); [Crossref query: “Geometric Efficiency Factor”](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=%22Geometric%20Efficiency%20Factor%22&rows=20)).

5. “Geometric/geometrical efficiency factor” already has unrelated technical uses, including radiation-detector solid-angle efficiency; using **GEF** without a qualifier would invite confusion and should be avoided ([Applied Radiation and Isotopes search result on defined-solid-angle counting](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969804315303109); [DOI:10.1016/0029-554X(67)91367-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0029-554X(67)91367-5)).

6. The strongest defensible novelty is therefore **not** invention of material reduction through design. It is a proposed **function-normalized, geometry-specific, behavior-aware, packaging/logistics-aware quantitative framework for short-lived consumables**, demonstrated across multiple cases with break-even and failure regions. That contribution must be positioned as a specialization and synthesis of established material-efficiency, eco-design, source-reduction, packaging-optimization, and rebound literatures ([Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002); [Willskytt 2021, DOI:10.3390/su13031036](https://doi.org/10.3390/su13031036); [Castro et al. 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136); [Wever 2011, DOI:10.1002/pts.927](https://doi.org/10.1002/pts.927)).

### Inference

The manuscript is most likely to survive skeptical review if “FGO” is introduced as a **study-defined analytical lens**, not a newly discovered sustainability principle or mature discipline. A safe formulation is: “We use *functional geometry optimization* to denote geometry-focused material-efficiency interventions that minimize material or scenario environmental burden per functional unit subject to performance, acceptance, manufacturing, compatibility, safety, and regulatory constraints” ([ISO 14044 functional-unit reference](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html); [Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002); [Ardente & Mathieux 2014, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2014.07.058](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2014.07.058)).

### Unresolved

The searches were targeted, not a formal systematic review; absence of an exact phrase cannot prove worldwide non-use. Claims of “first,” “novel,” or “unprecedented” remain unsupported until database-specific search strings, date ranges, deduplication, screening, and patent results are documented ([PRISMA 2020, DOI:10.1136/bmj.n71](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n71)).

## 3. Distinctions that should be explicit

### Material substitution

Substitution changes material composition; FGO changes supplied dimensions, segmentation, or spatial allocation. Either can change manufacturing, performance, use behavior, packaging, and end-of-life, so the \(2\times2\) comparison—baseline geometry/material, optimized geometry/current material, current geometry/substitute, optimized geometry/substitute—is conceptually sound. Published straw LCAs show why substitution results depend on functional unit, geography, production, and end-of-life assumptions and cannot be inferred from material names alone ([Zanghelini et al. 2020, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2020.123070](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2020.123070); [JCLP multidimensional straw LCA, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2023.136716](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2023.136716)).

### Lightweighting/thinning

Lightweighting is already an established “use less material” strategy, usually changing mass, thickness, or material grade while preserving nominal form and technical performance. FGO should be distinguished by its focus on which **dimensions or spatial portions** contribute to function, including width, length, area, shape, minimum dispensing increment, symmetry, and accessory-container matching. The distinction is analytical rather than absolute because a geometry change can also be ordinary lightweighting ([Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002); [Mayencourt et al. 2025, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.144518](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.144518)).

### Reuse and recycling

Reuse and remanufacturing slow material flows; recycling closes loops; geometry optimization narrows input per service. They are complementary rather than mutually exclusive. The introduction should avoid a “design instead of circularity” straw man and instead argue for geometry optimization **before and alongside** substitution, reuse where feasible, and recycling ([Bocken et al. 2016, DOI:10.1080/21681015.2016.1172124](https://doi.org/10.1080/21681015.2016.1172124); [Geissdoerfer et al. 2017, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2016.12.048](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2016.12.048)).

### Source reduction and waste prevention

FGO is one mechanism of source reduction/waste prevention, not a replacement term. The study contributes when it quantifies whether a proposed geometry actually reduces material per successful function after compensation, manufacturing losses, rejects, and replacement are included ([US EPA](https://www.epa.gov/p2/pollution-prevention-act-1990); [EU Waste Framework Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02008L0098-20251016)).

### Demand reduction

FGO usually reduces material intensity per delivered service; demand reduction reduces the number or level of services demanded. A narrower toilet-paper roll can save area per event while leaving sanitation-service demand unchanged, but compensation may increase longitudinal demand. The manuscript should therefore reserve “demand reduction” for an explicitly defined demand—material demand, item demand, or service demand—and not use it unqualified ([Allwood et al. 2013, DOI:10.1098/rsta.2012.0496](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2012.0496); [Skelton et al. 2020, DOI:10.1016/j.ecolecon.2019.106544](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolecon.2019.106544)).

### Dematerialization

A mass reduction is not sufficient evidence of dematerialization if functionality, lifespan, replacement, or material burden changes. Report material per functional unit first, then use “dematerialization” only for a documented service-normalized throughput reduction; reserve “environmental benefit” for a defensible lifecycle or scenario conversion ([van der Voet et al. 2004, DOI:10.1162/1088198043630432](https://doi.org/10.1162/1088198043630432); [Kasulaitis et al. 2019, DOI:10.1111/jiec.12756](https://doi.org/10.1111/jiec.12756)).

### Packaging optimization

Packaging literature already addresses volume optimization, product-to-package ratio, space utilization, and logistics. The new angle is to couple **product functional geometry** to package geometry and integer packing while distinguishing mass-constrained from volume-constrained transport. No proportional transport-emission saving should be inferred from mass reduction alone ([Wever 2011, DOI:10.1002/pts.927](https://doi.org/10.1002/pts.927); [García-Arca et al. 2020, DOI:10.3390/su12051870](https://doi.org/10.3390/su12051870)).

### Product miniaturization

“Miniaturization” should not be the preferred term because it says that an object becomes smaller but does not state the service denominator, environmental objective, binding constraint, or behavioral response. A geometry-focused material-efficiency formulation is more precise ([van der Voet et al. 2004, DOI:10.1162/1088198043630432](https://doi.org/10.1162/1088198043630432); [Liedtke et al. 2014, DOI:10.3390/resources3030544](https://doi.org/10.3390/resources3030544)).

## 4. FGO, GUE and GEF assessment

### Functional Geometry Optimization

**Search finding:** targeted Crossref and web exact-phrase searches did not identify a recognized cleaner-production framework named “Functional Geometry Optimization” ([Crossref exact-phrase query](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=%22Functional%20Geometry%20Optimization%22&rows=20)).

**Closest established equivalents:**

1. designing products with less material under the material-efficiency literature ([Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002));
2. material input per service unit/MIPS ([Liedtke et al. 2014, DOI:10.3390/resources3030544](https://doi.org/10.3390/resources3030544));
3. geometry/volume optimization in packaging ([Wever 2011, DOI:10.1002/pts.927](https://doi.org/10.1002/pts.927));
4. shape, size, topology, or structural optimization under performance constraints ([Mayencourt et al. 2025, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.144518](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.144518));
5. narrowing resource loops in circular design ([Bocken et al. 2016, DOI:10.1080/21681015.2016.1172124](https://doi.org/10.1080/21681015.2016.1172124)); and
6. resource-efficient design of consumables, a comparatively underdeveloped design domain ([Willskytt 2021, DOI:10.3390/su13031036](https://doi.org/10.3390/su13031036)).

**Recommendation:** retain FGO only if the paper defines it operationally and immediately locates it within those antecedents. Prefer the subtitle/descriptor “function-constrained geometry optimization for material efficiency” in methods and keywords because it is self-explanatory and searchable ([Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002); [ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html)).

### Geometry Utilization Efficiency

No established general sustainability indicator named GUE was identified in targeted exact-phrase searches ([Crossref exact-phrase query](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=%22Geometry%20Utilization%20Efficiency%22&rows=20)). Its closest concepts are MIPS, material utilization/yield, design utilization, and material efficiency, but each uses a different numerator, denominator, or system boundary ([Liedtke et al. 2014, DOI:10.3390/resources3030544](https://doi.org/10.3390/resources3030544); [Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002)).

The proposed ratio

\[
\mathrm{GUE} =
\frac{M_{\min,\ \mathrm{functionally\ feasible\ under\ stated\ constraints}}}
     {M_{\mathrm{supplied\ baseline}}}
\]

is not observable without defining the functional unit, optimization domain, functional threshold, behavior model, material definition, manufacturing tolerance, compatibility, safety, and regulatory constraints. Different valid constraint sets will give different “minimum” masses; therefore GUE should be labelled a **study-specific scenario diagnostic**, not a universal property of a product ([ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html); [Ardente & Mathieux 2014, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2014.07.058](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2014.07.058)).

At minimum, report separate values:

- theoretical GUE under physical geometry only;
- engineering-feasible GUE under manufacturing and compatibility constraints;
- behaviorally feasible GUE under acceptance, compensation, failure, and replacement assumptions; and
- observed GUE only if empirical use data support it ([Castro et al. 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136); [Lowe et al. 2024, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.141063](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.141063)).

### Geometric Efficiency Factor

Avoid **GEF** as the headline name. Besides being opaque, “geometrical efficiency factor” is used in detector physics for solid-angle/counting efficiency, making disciplinary collision likely ([ScienceDirect: defined-solid-angle counter](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969804315303109); [DOI:10.1016/0029-554X(67)91367-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0029-554X(67)91367-5)).

If \(1-\mathrm{GUE}\) is retained, call it a **study-defined geometric excess fraction**, state the exact baseline and constraint set in every table/figure, and do not imply that it is an established sustainability metric. Even then, “excess” should mean excess relative to the stated optimization problem, not wasteful intent or universally unnecessary material ([ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html); [van der Voet et al. 2004, DOI:10.1162/1088198043630432](https://doi.org/10.1162/1088198043630432)).

## 5. Geometry–function–behavior–system map

The causal chain should be modelled rather than assumed:

\[
\text{geometry}
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{unit mass/area/volume}\\
\text{functional performance and failure}\\
\text{manufacturability and yield}\\
\text{compatibility and acceptance}\\
\text{package dimensions and integer packing}
\end{cases}
\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{use quantity, compensation, replacement}\\
\text{production and packaging input/FU}\\
\text{mass- or volume-constrained logistics/FU}\\
\text{waste and end-of-life flows/FU}
\end{cases}
\rightarrow
\text{scenario environmental burden/FU}.
\]

This system framing is necessary because resource-efficient product measures can create trade-offs across lifecycle stages, and circular/resource-efficiency rebound can offset direct savings ([Ardente & Mathieux 2014, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2014.07.058](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2014.07.058); [Castro et al. 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136)).

### Functional unit and reference flow

The defensible toilet-paper functional unit is not “one sheet” or “one roll”; it is a successful use event meeting predefined adequacy and hygiene criteria, with product area or mass required per event as the reference flow. The defensible straw functional unit is a successfully consumed beverage serving meeting defined liquid-access, residual-liquid, leakage, usability, and failure criteria. These are proposed study definitions, not established standards, and require validation or transparent scenario assumptions ([ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html)).

### Behavioral compensation

For toilet paper, the exact geometric saving from reducing width survives only if the longitudinal-consumption ratio remains below the inverse width ratio:

\[
\frac{L_{\mathrm{new}}}{L_{\mathrm{old}}}
<
\frac{W_{\mathrm{old}}}{W_{\mathrm{new}}}.
\]

This is an analytical identity from \(A=W L\), not an empirical result. The distribution of compensation is unresolved until measured or explicitly scenario-modelled; rebound reviews support treating direct, indirect, and system mechanisms as empirical questions rather than assuming zero compensation ([Castro et al. 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136); [Metic & Pigosso 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.133133](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.133133)).

For straws, reduced length can be offset by difficult positioning, incomplete access, residual liquid, buckling, leakage, or replacement. Manufacturer information confirms that commercial straw length and diameter depend on package size/shape and beverage viscosity, supporting joint container–accessory design rather than a length-only model ([Tetra Pak straw systems](https://www.tetrapak.com/en-ke/solutions/packaging/openings-and-closures/straws)).

### Packaging and logistics

A material saving changes transport only through explicit payload or cube constraints, packaging redesign, integer packing, and utilization. Report both kilograms and cubic metres per 1,000 successful functions, plus functional uses per pallet/vehicle under stated loading assumptions; do not convert mass savings directly into transport-emission savings ([Wever 2011, DOI:10.1002/pts.927](https://doi.org/10.1002/pts.927); [García-Arca et al. 2020, DOI:10.3390/su12051870](https://doi.org/10.3390/su12051870)).

### End of life and environmental claims

Reduced input or waste mass is not automatically reduced lifecycle impact. Material production, conversion, package changes, transport, failure/replacement, and end-of-life scenarios must be included to claim scenario environmental benefit; otherwise use “material-flow reduction” or “source-reduction potential.” LCA-based circular-design literature also reports inconsistent integration of LCA into design processes, supporting transparent boundaries and uncertainty rather than broad impact claims ([Falsafi et al. 2025, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2025.146188](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2025.146188); [ISO 14040](https://www.iso.org/standard/37456.html); [ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html)).

## 6. Case-relevant literature implications

### Disposable consumables

Consumables merit explicit treatment rather than simply transferring durable-product strategies. Willskytt's review found that less than half of general resource-efficient design recommendations considered were applicable to consumables, identifying a relevant research gap for tissues, wipes, straws, and similar short-lived products ([Willskytt 2021, DOI:10.3390/su13031036](https://doi.org/10.3390/su13031036)).

### Toilet-paper width and center perforation

The reviewed cleaner-production literature establishes the general principles but does not supply empirical width–consumption elasticity or task-level adequacy distributions. Until direct evidence is acquired, width compensation and half-width selection frequencies must be labelled hypothetical scenarios, and “50% saving” must not be stated for center perforation because users can select multiple half-width units and tasks are heterogeneous ([ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html); [Castro et al. 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136)).

The stronger conceptual contribution is **two-dimensional discretization of consumption**: reducing mismatch between required functional area and the minimum dispensed area. This should be presented as a study hypothesis linked to material efficiency and source reduction, not as an established named theory ([Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002); [US EPA](https://www.epa.gov/p2/pollution-prevention-act-1990)).

### Straw–container co-design

Peer-reviewed straw LCAs provide substitution comparators but do not establish that shortening is beneficial for every container or material. A geometry study should use observed configurations and model length jointly with container geometry, insertion point, access, protrusion, residual liquid, stiffness/failure, usability, and replacement. Tetra Pak's design description supports package size/shape and viscosity as relevant design parameters; comparative LCA supports explicit geography, system boundary, and end-of-life assumptions ([Tetra Pak](https://www.tetrapak.com/en-ke/solutions/packaging/openings-and-closures/straws); [Zanghelini et al. 2020, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2020.123070](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2020.123070)).

## 8. Conservative novelty language

### Recommended wording

> This study does not propose material reduction through design as a new principle. It develops and tests a geometry-specific, function-normalized extension of material-efficiency and eco-design thinking for disposable consumables. The framework couples supplied geometry to functional adequacy, behavioral compensation, manufacturing and compatibility constraints, packaging and logistics, and end-of-life scenarios, and reports break-even and failure regions rather than assuming that smaller geometry yields environmental benefit. We use “functional geometry optimization” as a study-defined descriptor, not as an established discipline. ([Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002); [Willskytt 2021, DOI:10.3390/su13031036](https://doi.org/10.3390/su13031036); [Castro et al. 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136); [Wever 2011, DOI:10.1002/pts.927](https://doi.org/10.1002/pts.927)).

### Claims to avoid

- Avoid “the first study” or “a novel sustainability principle”; targeted searches cannot establish exhaustive priority ([PRISMA 2020, DOI:10.1136/bmj.n71](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n71)).
- Avoid “geometry is overlooked” without a scoped qualifier because packaging, topology, shape, and structural optimization literatures already optimize geometry ([Wever 2011, DOI:10.1002/pts.927](https://doi.org/10.1002/pts.927); [Mayencourt et al. 2025, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.144518](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.144518)).
- Avoid “material savings equal environmental savings” because dematerialization and LCA literatures require service equivalence and lifecycle accounting ([van der Voet et al. 2004, DOI:10.1162/1088198043630432](https://doi.org/10.1162/1088198043630432); [ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html)).
- Avoid “50% saving” for center-perforated toilet paper before modelling selection frequencies and compensation ([Castro et al. 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136)).
- Avoid presenting GUE/GEF as validated or standardized indicators; no such status was identified, and GEF is terminologically overloaded ([Crossref GUE query](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=%22Geometry%20Utilization%20Efficiency%22&rows=20); [detector-physics usage](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969804315303109)).

## 9. Recommended actions for the coordinating study

1. **Use established terms before introducing FGO.** Lead with material efficiency, eco-design, source reduction, waste prevention, and narrowing resource flows; define FGO as the geometry-focused analytical subset ([Allwood et al. 2011, DOI:10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2010.11.002); [Bocken et al. 2016, DOI:10.1080/21681015.2016.1172124](https://doi.org/10.1080/21681015.2016.1172124)).

2. **Retain GUE only as a scenario-indexed diagnostic.** Every value should name the functional unit, baseline, geometry domain, constraint set, behavior model, and whether it is theoretical, engineering-feasible, behaviorally feasible, or observed ([ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html); [Ardente & Mathieux 2014, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2014.07.058](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2014.07.058)).

3. **Drop “GEF” as the preferred abbreviation.** If a complement is useful, write “geometric excess fraction, \(1-\mathrm{GUE}\), study-defined” and avoid standalone claims of inefficiency ([detector-physics collision](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0969804315303109)).

4. **Predefine service-level functional units and reference flows.** Use successful toilet-paper use events and successful beverage servings only after specifying measurable adequacy/usability criteria; report material and package volume per 1,000 successful functions as secondary metrics ([ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html)).

5. **Make behavior a constraint and uncertainty, not a discussion afterthought.** Derive exact compensation/replacement break-even boundaries and report robust-benefit, conditional-benefit, and no-benefit/harm regions ([Castro et al. 2022, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.131136); [Lowe et al. 2024, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.141063](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.141063)).

6. **Treat packaging/logistics as a coupled integer problem.** Model unchanged packaging and optimized repacking separately, under mass- and cube-constrained vehicles, and do not infer transport emissions directly from item mass ([Wever 2011, DOI:10.1002/pts.927](https://doi.org/10.1002/pts.927); [García-Arca et al. 2020, DOI:10.3390/su12051870](https://doi.org/10.3390/su12051870)).

7. **Use “material-flow reduction” for the primary result.** Escalate to “scenario environmental burden reduction” only where sourced coefficients and lifecycle stages support it; do not label the partial analysis a full LCA ([ISO 14040](https://www.iso.org/standard/37456.html); [ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html); [Falsafi et al. 2025, DOI:10.1016/j.jclepro.2025.146188](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2025.146188)).

8. **Do not transfer durable-product assumptions directly to consumables.** Use Willskytt's consumables gap as the rationale for product-specific function, failure, and use modelling ([Willskytt 2021, DOI:10.3390/su13031036](https://doi.org/10.3390/su13031036)).

9. **Complete a documented systematic search before priority claims.** Record databases, exact search strings, dates, screening criteria, exclusions, and patent results; a negative exact-phrase query alone supports only “not identified in our targeted search” ([PRISMA 2020, DOI:10.1136/bmj.n71](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.n71)).

## 10. Uncertainties and evidence gaps

1. No empirical toilet-paper width–longitudinal-consumption response, center-perforation selection distribution, or validated adequacy threshold was established in this literature task; those parameters require direct evidence or explicitly hypothetical sensitivity distributions ([ISO 14044](https://www.iso.org/standard/38498.html)).
2. No patent conclusion has been reached for divided or longitudinally perforated toilet paper; patent searching and legal-status checking remain separate tasks, and neither novelty nor freedom to operate can be inferred from this report ([WIPO PATENTSCOPE](https://patentscope.wipo.int/); [Espacenet](https://worldwide.espacenet.com/); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/)).
3. No universal minimum straw length exists in the checked sources; feasible length depends on package geometry, beverage properties, access and usability requirements, mechanical behavior, and replacement ([Tetra Pak](https://www.tetrapak.com/en-ke/solutions/packaging/openings-and-closures/straws)).
4. Full-text evidence appraisal was completed selectively rather than for all metadata records; the accompanying reference table should be treated as DOI metadata verification, not systematic risk-of-bias or claim-level verification ([Crossref REST API documentation](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/rest-api/)).
5. Current JCLP submission requirements were outside this specialist report and still require verification from Elsevier's official Guide for Authors before manuscript production ([Journal of Cleaner Production journal page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/journal-of-cleaner-production)).
