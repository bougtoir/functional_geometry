## 2. Is the surviving distinction substantive?

The prompt's test: *if the only difference is adding a subjective environmental score to an established fixation experiment, the contribution is insufficient.*

- **A rating-based E3 would fail this test.** Rating-based environmental outcomes for brief manipulations already exist (Liao 2021; Ruiz-Pastor 2024; Alzayed 2025).
- **E3 differs substantively only if all three of N6, N7 and N10 are delivered:**
  - **N6:** a quantitative, functional-unit-based impact estimate;
  - **N7:** functional equivalence as an observable outcome;
  - **N10:** demonstrated independent-assessor agreement and benchmark validity.

  Then E3 answers a question the rating-based studies cannot: whether the cue-driven content changes reported so far (more sustainability vocabulary, more circular features, higher rated relevance) correspond to **any difference in delivered environmental performance**. The prior nulls make this sharper, not weaker. E3 could show that ratings and impact diverge, or that the nulls persist under a magnitude endpoint.
- **If N10 fails** (assessor agreement or benchmark validity is inadequate), the contribution collapses to N5 + N8. That is incremental, and probably insufficient for an original research article.

**Boundary statement.** The only defensible contribution is:

> A controlled comparison of environmental design-brief emphasis with a functional-equivalence assessment and a quantitative environmental impact per successful functional unit for each participant's selected concept, using a pre-validated, blinded, two-assessor screening-LCA endpoint.

It is an **extension**. Novelty is **pending** Scopus/WoS closure and endpoint validation.
