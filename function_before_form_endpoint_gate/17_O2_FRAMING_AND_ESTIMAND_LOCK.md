# 17 — O2 Framing and Estimand Lock (working design)

## 1. Estimand (locked)

**Intention-to-treat (ITT) effect of the *assigned* brief emphasis** (A material substitution, B form-preserving improvement, C function-first, form-unconstrained) on the endpoint of the concept each participant submits as final, under an identical permitted design space, information pack and effort budget.

- It is *not* the effect of complying with the emphasis, or of the architecture actually chosen.
- Primary contrasts: C vs A and C vs B, two-sided. A vs B is secondary.
- **Not conditioned on:**
  - functional success (FE status is a component of the primary outcome, see 18 §3);
  - number of ideas generated;
  - manipulation-check responses;
  - concept completeness.
- Every randomised participant contributes. A missing final concept is analysed as the worst FE category (18 §3).

## 2. Brief structure (identical except §2.2)

### 2.1 Common block (verbatim in all arms)

1. The service to be delivered, stated in functional-unit terms (18 §1), plus the FE requirements F1–F8 in plain language.
2. The environmental objective: "Your goal is to propose the concept that would deliver this service with the lowest overall environmental impact over its whole life cycle."
3. **Explicit permission:** "Any technically feasible material, form, product, reuse system, service or delivery arrangement is permitted. You are not required to keep the existing product's shape, material or way of working."
4. An identical reference-product description and photo for all arms. Showing it to only some arms would add an example-exposure confound.
5. An identical environmental information sheet: one page, soft information, no LCA tables. This choice follows Collado-Ruiz 2010, where detailed LCA information depressed creativity (pp. 489–492). Holding it constant removes the information confound.
6. Identical time (fixed ideation + selection + documentation), worksheet (06 template) and materials.

### 2.2 Framing paragraph (the only difference)

The paragraphs are matched on word count (±5%), reading level, number of sentences and number of example cues. None contains numbers or environmental data. Draft paragraphs are to be refined and pre-tested; the content below is illustrative:

- **A:** "Many environmental improvements come from choosing better materials. Consider which materials could deliver this service with less impact."
- **B:** "Many environmental improvements come from refining an existing design: its geometry, mass, construction and how it is made. Consider how the existing product could be improved."
- **C:** "Many environmental improvements come from rethinking how a need is met. Consider the service itself and the different ways it could be delivered."

## 3. Strength versus constraint

- **Risk 1: manipulation too weak.** The common permission sentence may override the emphasis.
  - Evidence that brief-level cues are weak: Ruiz-Pastor 2024 and Alzayed 2025 both found nulls for brief objectives and goals.
  - Mitigation: a *non-outcome* manipulation-strength pilot (the architecture-class distribution of concepts, coded blind). This requires separate participants, so it **is a human-subject study and needs ethics review; it is not performed here.**
  - If emphasis does not shift architecture classes, the main study is unlikely to be informative.
- **Risk 2: emphasis acts as an implicit constraint.** Participants may read A or B as rules.
  - Mitigation: the permission sentence is placed *after* the framing paragraph, and the instructions state "the paragraph above is a suggestion, not a requirement".
  - A post-task item asks whether they believed any options were disallowed (§4).

## 4. Manipulation checks that do not alter the treatment

All are administered **after** the final concept is locked:
1. "Which aspect did the brief encourage you to consider most?" (material / design refinement / way of meeting the need / none).
2. "Did you believe any materials, forms or systems were not allowed?" (yes/no plus text). This is a **constraint-perception check**.
3. Blind coding of each final concept's architecture class (same form, same material / same form, new material / new form / system or service). This is a behavioural check and also a secondary outcome.

Checks are used descriptively and in sensitivity analyses only, **never to exclude participants from the ITT analysis**.

## 5. Hypothesis guessing

- The cover description is "a study of how designers respond to different brief formats". The arms and hypotheses are not disclosed.
- Each participant sees one arm only (between-subjects).
- A funnel debrief item asks: "What do you think the study was testing?" Answers are coded blind.
- A pre-specified sensitivity analysis excludes participants who correctly identify the framing contrast. It is reported alongside the ITT analysis, not instead of it.

## 6. Status

The O2 design is **specified sufficiently for planning**. The framing paragraphs are drafts, and their equivalence (length, readability) and strength are **untested**.
