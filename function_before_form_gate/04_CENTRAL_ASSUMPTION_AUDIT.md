# 04 — Central Assumption Audit (Gate C)

Central assumption examined: *environmental product development over-emphasises substituting the material while keeping a familiar physical form.*

Labels used: **Directly supported by empirical evidence** / **Supported by established theory** / **Plausible but untested** / **Contradicted by available evidence** / **Not established**.

| # | Sub-claim | Evidence found | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Professional designers/firms predominantly respond to environmental goals by material substitution with form fixed | No frequency study of designer/firm strategy choice found (query s09 returned no usable study). Ceschin & Gaziulusoy (FT) describe *early* green design as focused on redesigning product qualities, including material replacement, as a historical characterisation, not a measured frequency. | **Not established** |
| C2 | Consumers judge environmental performance by material type | Boesen et al. 2019: consumers assess packaging "primarily based on the material type" and disposal; Sokolova et al. 2023: eight studies, N = 4,103, "paper = good, plastic = bad" bias affects choice | **Directly supported by empirical evidence** (demand side only) |
| C3 | Policy (bans/taxes on a material) induces like-for-like substitution | Abate & Elofsson 2024 model bans/taxes leading consumers to "similar unregulated substitutes" | **Supported by established theory**; magnitude in practice not assessed here |
| C4 | Like-for-like material substitution can shift or increase burdens | Kakadellis & Harris 2020 (FT, 19 LCAs); Gallego-Schmid et al. 2019; Stefanini et al. 2021; Zimmerman & Anastas 2015 | **Directly supported by empirical evidence** (case-specific LCAs) — and already published |
| C5 | Function-first practice is already standard | Normatively standard in design methodology (S01–S04) and in LCA functional units (S32); FIM exists (S26). Actual industrial uptake of ecodesign tools is reported as limited (Devanathan abstract; Faludi roadmap abstract) | **Supported by established theory** (as norm); practice uptake **not established** |
| C6 | Fixed form is often rational, not a bias | Regulatory/food-contact approval, safety, existing filling/dispensing infrastructure, user acceptance, switching cost and lock-in (Arthur 1989; Unruh 2000) are established explanations; Crilly & Cardoso 2017 note repetition of features may have benefits | **Supported by established theory**; it directly competes with the "bias" interpretation |
| C7 | Reopening form/delivery system yields better environmental outcomes | Tukker 2004: "most PSS types will result in marginal environmental improvements at best"; Kjaer et al. 2019: PSS "no implicit guarantee"; Gallego-Schmid: reuse better only after 3–39 reuses; Zahner 2010: abstraction yields ideas that violate constraints | **Contradicted as a general claim**; true only conditionally |
| C8 | Redesign without substitution (geometry, lightweighting, portioning) is an under-used route | Lightweighting/material efficiency literature exists (Allwood et al. 2011, MD; t12 results); no comparative frequency data | **Plausible but untested** |
| C9 | Environmental marketing over-weights material claims relative to engineering practice | Consumer-perception literature (C2) supports demand-side material salience; no study separating marketing claims from engineering decisions found | **Plausible but untested** |
| C10 | Material-first briefs cause designers to retain form (fixation pathway) | Fixation literature shows examples and first ideas anchor features (S05–S12); no study manipulating a *material* brief found | **Plausible but untested** |

## Falsifiers (what would show the assumption is wrong)

1. A representative sample of eco-redesign projects (e.g., EPD-backed product revisions or ecodesign case databases) in which form/function changes are as frequent as material substitution.
2. A framing experiment in which a material-substitution brief produces concepts with the same or lower screening-LCA impact per functional unit than a functional brief, at equal effort.
3. Evidence that observed form retention is fully explained by regulatory, safety or infrastructure constraints (rational retention rather than bias).

## Gate C conclusion

The "substitution bias" premise is **empirically demonstrated only on the consumer side** and in the form of known burden-shifting case LCAs. On the designer/firm side it is **not established**. A study cannot assume it; it would have to measure it (C1, C10) or avoid depending on it. The narrow question carried forward (Gate E, route E3) is framed so that it tests C10 directly and does not presuppose C1.
